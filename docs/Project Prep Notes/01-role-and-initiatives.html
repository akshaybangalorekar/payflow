The Role, Decoded

"Senior Engineering resource with strong Technical Lead and Solution Design experience." That is a person who owns solution designs, runs technical assessments, sits in design assurance reviews, keeps vendor integrations honest, and carries release readiness. Not a coder. The hiring manager confirmed it: no hands-on expected. What they are buying is judgment plus the ability to lead engineers who do the hands-on.

The Four Initiatives
Where the four initiatives sit, and where your experience lands hardest (right side plus the replacement pattern).
Initiative 1: Digital

The catch-all. Bank-wide digital initiatives means new customer-facing capability delivered through modern integration: APIs, events, and gradual replacement of legacy paths. This is where design assurance and architecture governance from the JD get exercised daily.

Your anchor: at Westpac you watched the enterprise integration story move from EIG, the legacy integration capability focused on ETL, batch and file transformation, to EBIP, its replacement focused on API services, real-time event integration and enterprise orchestration. The stated reason, from your own notes: without a common platform every system builds point-to-point integrations, tight coupling, duplicated work, delivery risk. With it, each system integrates once. That sentence is a design-assurance answer waiting to be asked.

Initiative 2: ITTR Replacement

ITTR is the Information Technology Technical Refresh programme: replacing ageing technology across the estate, end-of-support platforms, outdated vendor product versions, and the infrastructure underneath them. Nothing public documents this bank's exact ITTR scope, so treat the boundaries as a question to confirm, but the shape of a technical refresh is well known: keep the business running while the ground under it is replaced.

Why that is good news for you: this is the closest thing in the whole JD to your telecom years plus your Westpac consolidation. You led gateway teams through a platform migration at Kapsch (ATCA to Kairos), kept carrier-grade charging platforms upgraded under sustained production load at Ericsson, and at Westpac moved 325,000 accounts across platforms with identity continuity, wave sequencing and reconciliation. A refresh programme is judged on one thing: nothing breaks that used to work. That is regression discipline, contract preservation and cutover safety, all of which you have done.

The refresh lifecycle you would walk them through, from your modernization rubric, your Kapsch and Ericsson platform upgrades, and your Gemini experience.
If Asked How You Would Approach ITTR "A technical refresh has one success criterion: same behaviour on new technology, provably. So my first questions are the rubric: what's end-of-support, what's the coupling, where's the data gravity. Then the discipline: freeze the integration contracts so consumers don't churn while the platform under them changes, prove equivalence with parallel run and reconciliation rather than assertion, cut over in waves with a rollback path for each wave, and only decommission the old estate when the numbers say the new one is proven. I've lived the risky half of that at Westpac, moving 325,000 accounts with identity continuity and reconciliation, and the platform-upgrade half in telecom, keeping charging platforms alive under production load while they were refreshed underneath me."
Initiative 3: Mobile Banking Apps

Feature delivery into the bank's mobile apps: payments features, account servicing, wallet integration. The backend work is BFF and API layer design, plus the integrations behind each feature.

Your anchor, from your NAB portfolio: ADI internal transfer and BPAY out (moving money between accounts and paying bills from the app), PayAll on Direct Entry (everyday scheduled payments with rewards, riding the batch rail), and USL Value Post (payment posting from partners and NAB accounts). You also established a multi-channel digital delivery model across the wallet platforms. So when they say mobile banking apps, your answer is: I designed the payments features and the wallet rails those apps sit on.

Initiative 4: GPAY / VCAS

This is your home ground. Two pieces: Google Pay issuer enablement, which is your NAB wallet work with Google as the token requestor, and VCAS, Visa Consumer Authentication Service, Visa's 3-D Secure issuer-side product with real-time risk-based authentication for card-not-present transactions.

VCAS specifics worth two evenings on the Visa developer portal: it is EMV 3DS compliant, it does risk-based authentication so low-risk checkouts pass frictionlessly and high-risk ones get challenged, and it exposes a Card Status API (real-time PAN status control, replacing manual portal actions and legacy SFTP files) and a Confirmed Marking API (where the issuer reports a transaction as Good, Fraud, or Undetermined). Visa's published Australian issuer case study: moving from SMS OTP-only to risk-based authentication cut challenge rates 48 percent, reduced abandonment by 113 basis points and fraud by 6 basis points.
