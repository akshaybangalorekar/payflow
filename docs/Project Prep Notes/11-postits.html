The post-it wall

The lines that actually have to come out of your mouth on Thursday. One card here is one post-it. Each is sized so you can hand-write it without cramming — a heading, one line to say, and a short note about when to say it. Colour-coded so you can group them on a wall or a desk.

How to use this page

Do not write all of them. Start with the eight below, in the colour they are listed under. If you have time and paper left, work down the wall. Write the big line in your own handwriting and skip the small print — the small print is there so you remember what the card is for, not so you copy it.

If you only write eight
  1. "The cloud realisation I can design and defend, but I haven't operated it at that scale — I'll flag which is which as we go."
  2. "Synchronous because a payment is waiting on it. Asynchronous because nothing else is."
  3. "A timeout is a business decision with a loss rate, not an error."
  4. "Authorisation is a promise. Clearing is the confirmation. Settlement is the money moving."
  5. "The network runs the machinery. The issuer owns the decision."
  6. "The gateway can tell you which client is calling. It can never tell you whose record this is."
  7. 350 TPS · p99 ~100ms · under 0.1% timeouts · A$0.70 per A$1,000.
  8. "I don't know that one — but structurally it has to sit either with the scheme or with the issuer."

Open with this
the framing, first two minutes
"My hands-on delivery is API and integration architecture in payments. The cloud realisation I can design and defend, but I haven't operated it at that scale — and I'll flag which is which as we go."
Volunteering the boundary early buys you credibility on everything after it.
tell me about yourself — three beats, then stop
Twenty-two years. Seventeen in carrier-grade telecom. Five in Australian banking — card fraud, tokenisation, disputes, then a collections platform consolidation.
Ninety seconds. Then stop talking and let them choose the thread.
why this role
"Two of your four initiatives are things I've designed for on the issuing side, and the third is a platform replacement, which is what I spent last year on."
Specific beats enthusiastic. Say it in one breath.
the two halves — use it every technical answer
"That part I lived. This next part is how I'd build it on AWS — design reasoning, not something I've operated."
This single habit protects every other claim you make.
The numbers
the only set you use
350 TPS sustained · p99 ~100 ms · under 0.1% timeouts · fraud loss under A$0.70 per A$1,000.
Your notes hold two other sets. Never mix them — that is how you sound like you are quoting someone else's platform.
the arithmetic they may ask for
Concurrency = throughput × latency. 350 a second at 100 ms is 35 in flight. So three instances across three zones — for the failure domain, not the throughput.
Say which number you'd want to measure rather than assume. That is the senior move.
what was set at the edge and the gateway
Roughly 2,000 requests per 5 minutes from one source · 32 KB body cap · 500 a second per partner, burst 1,000 · 5,000 a second for the whole stage.
Say "roughly". A rounded real number beats a precise invented one.
partition arithmetic
700 a second, one consumer handles a couple of hundred → about four. So start at twelve, because the count is effectively fixed and it caps your consumers.
Doing the division out loud is worth more than the answer.
Architecture
the split, and the root of every answer
"Synchronous because a payment is waiting on it. Asynchronous because nothing else is."
If you get lost mid-answer, come back to this sentence.
why the events exist at all
"The events feed the state that the fast path reads."
The counters are precomputed and looked up, never calculated inside the budget.
timeouts
"A timeout is a business decision with a loss rate, not an error. The caller owns it, and it has to resolve to a decision — never to an error page."
If you did not decide it in advance, you delegated it to whoever deployed last.
degradation
"Fewer signals means stricter decisions. Degraded mode tightens — it does not relax."
Otherwise you have published a recipe: knock over the vendor, get everything approved.
an incident, first five minutes
"If the latency is dependency-driven, scaling makes it worse. Stabilise first, scale second."
Shed load, degrade the optional signals, cap vendor concurrency. Then scale.
streams
"Key by the account, because ordering only exists inside a partition — and the account is the thing that never changes."
Not the payee, not the channel, not whoever is handling the case.
delivery guarantees
"At-least-once delivery plus idempotent processing. Exactly-once stops the moment you write outside the broker."
Anyone promising exactly-once across systems is describing something narrower than it sounds.
the shape of every design answer
The constraint. Two real options. The choice, in one sentence. What it cost, and what would make you revisit it.
Almost nobody offers the fourth beat. It is the highest-scoring clause available to you.
Payments
the three stages
"Authorisation is a promise. Clearing is the confirmation. Settlement is the money moving."
A hold is not a charge. It expires if nobody clears it.
who is who
"The acquirer is the merchant's bank. The issuer is the customer's bank. The decision is always the issuer's."
The acquirer acquires the merchant. That is how you remember it.
tokens
"The token is domain restricted and it outlives the card. The issuer owns the provisioning decision even though the wallet holds the token."
A stolen token is a key to a door that only opens for one lock.
the sentence that covers wallets and 3DS at once
"The network runs the machinery. The issuer owns the decision."
Say this first in any wallet or authentication question. It frames everything after it.
disputes
"Allocation is automated liability assignment. Collaboration is an evidence exchange."
And every arrow has a deadline — miss one and liability lands regardless of merit.
3-D secure, in one line
"Low risk goes through invisibly. High risk gets challenged. Every challenge costs sales, so the bank wants to authenticate well, not often."
That tension is the whole design.
your own wallet line
"Three wallets, three separate identity checks, one issuer-side orchestration model — and the design had to carry which wallet vouched for this."
This is the strongest sentence you own on the GPAY initiative.
Security & governance
the distinction they will test
"mTLS says who is on the connection. OAuth says what the software may do. Neither says whose record this is."
That third check lives in the service, on every read.
object-level authorization
"The gateway can tell you which client is calling. It can never tell you whose record this is."
Never query by the identifier alone — always by the identifier and the caller's own scope.
design assurance
"Not 'did you follow the rules'. Is this safe to build, and can you defend it to the people who'll have to run it."
Four things in any design: the constraint, the rejected option, the failure behaviour, who is on the hook at 3am.
vendor products
"You can't assure the inside, so you assure the boundary." SLA, versioning notice, behaviour when they are down, who owns the data.
Without a boundary layer, their next release becomes your outage.
release readiness
"That we know how we'd undo it — and that we decided the abort condition before go-live night."
The abort condition is the part people skip, and it is the part that saves the night.
Honesty — the answers that survive a follow-up
the security pattern
"I didn't originate it — there was already a reference design in the bank. What I owned was making it real for a specific estate, and that turned out to be the harder half."
Claiming authorship does not survive a follow-up. This does.
which broker
"I'd choose on the estate rather than on merit — whichever the platform team already runs well."
Your resume does not name one. Do not invent one, and do not claim hands-on Kafka or MSK.
containers and cloud platforms
"I design for it. I haven't operated it. I'd rather say that now than have you find it in week three."
Kubernetes, OpenShift, Azure, Terraform. Short, calm, then move on.
java
"About eight years of core Java, a while ago. I read it and review it; I don't write it daily. If you want someone who'll out-code your team, that isn't me."
Then finish on what you will do: argue with the team about a design and understand their answer.
when you don't know
"I don't know that specific one — but structurally it has to sit either with the scheme or with the issuer, and given it affects liability I'd expect it to be a scheme rule."
Reasoning out loud from a model you clearly hold beats a guess, every time.
ITTR
"I've read it as a technology refresh replacement — but I'd rather you tell me the actual scope than have me guess."
There is no public definition of it. Asking is the correct answer.
Never say
never
"We ran it on MSK."
The next question is your partition count and your consumer rebalancing story, and the interview ends there.
never
Your old employers' internal programme and system names.
Say "the collections platform", "the legacy card ledger", "the integration platform", "the credit bureau feed".
never
Specific fraud rules, thresholds or detection signals.
That is exploitable information about a live bank. Describe the shape of the decision, never the values.
never
"Nothing — I'd do it the same way again."
Have one real thing you'd change. Yours: build the reconciliation and drift reporting before any data moves.
check this before Thursday
The CBA Centre of Excellence claim.
If you cannot defend it for three minutes, drop it and lead with Westpac and NAB. One soft claim costs you the hard ones.
Ask them
ask
"What's actually in scope for the ITTR replacement — the platform, the infrastructure underneath it, or both?"
You need this answer, and asking it proves you know the difference.
ask
"Is the VCAS work replacing an existing 3DS solution, and is the integration direct or through the processor?"
Only somebody who has worked the issuing side asks this. Use it right after you admit you haven't integrated VCAS.
ask
"How much of the estate is vendor product versus built in-house?"
It changes what solution design means day to day, and they will know that you know.
ask
"Who signs off a design here, and what does that forum actually look like?"
Design assurance is a third of this role. Ask how it really works.
Thursday morning

Take the eight. Read them in the car park, put them in your pocket, and do not open anything else. You will walk in with eight sentences instead of eleven pages, and that is the right trade.
