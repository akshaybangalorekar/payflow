My Banking Story: NAB, Westpac, CBA

Five years in Australian banking on top of seventeen in carrier-grade telecom. The same discipline the whole way: real-time decisions on a live transaction path, in regulated environments. Lead with Westpac (freshest), go deepest on NAB (technical), use CBA for governance.

Westpac: Gemini Collections (Most Recent)

How to quote your role: "Architecture owner for the Collections domain on Westpac's Gemini program, a post-acquisition multi-brand consolidation. Roughly 325,000 collections accounts across seven portfolios, business, wealth and SME."

The program structure: Aries proves it, Gemini scales it, Taurus industrialises it. Two chains run through the estate, and knowing they are separate is half the architecture. The migration lineage chain moves customer and account history from legacy ledgers to the target platforms. The collections servicing chain runs day-to-day collections. They intersect only at reporting.

The B2K Story (your flagship behavioral answer)

The equipment finance history was going to be routed through an interim mainframe because the target system was "assessed as not ready." You challenged the premise, went to the target system's own designer, established it could take the data directly, and proposed loading straight in with legacy-brand access handled by credential provisioning. Two migrations became one, the interim mainframe left the critical path, reconciliation burden roughly halved.

The Line To Say Out Loud "I converted a data migration problem into an access provisioning problem. The requirement was never 'the data must sit where the agents are'. It was 'the agents must be able to see the history'."

Risk story (if they ask what keeps you up at night)

The worst-case failure in the whole architecture: a re-age defect flows through to SRDE and out to the credit bureaus. Wrong data on someone's credit file. That is why irreversible operations need approval gates and why the migrated-flag risk was named and tracked, not assumed away.

The constraint that drove everything was external: a card authorisation has a scheme-mandated response budget. The issuer answers within a few hundred milliseconds or the transaction times out and goes to stand-in processing. Fraud got a slice of a budget somebody else set. That one fact splits the design in two.

figure 2 — one bounded synchronous call on top; everything that is not waiting lives underneath

Resilience detail that wins the backend developer

Signals were classified mandatory versus optional. Optional signals get skipped under load to protect tail latency. A missing mandatory signal triggers a safe fallback, step-up or MFA, rather than blowing out p99. Idempotency keys enforced before processing so client retries short-circuit. Correlation IDs through every hop. A timeout resolves to a defined fallback because a slow decline and a fast decline look identical to the cardholder but very different on your losses.

The near-real-time path

Digital, non-monetary requests came in as lightweight events on Kafka, were enriched in Lambda, scored, and published back as decision events. Same platform, different physics: event-driven because nothing downstream was waiting on the answer.

Wallets and disputes

Issuer wallet enablement across three trust boundaries, each wallet running its own ID&V before Visa Token Service issues a token. Disputes: VROL (Visa Resolve Online, the platform) implementing VCR (Visa Claims Resolution, the process): four categories, allocation (fraud, authorization: Visa decisioning assigns liability in real time) versus collaboration (processing errors, consumer disputes: evidence exchange), stages from dispute through representment, pre-arbitration to arbitration, and the genuinely non-obvious part you owned: FX variance between transaction and resolution amounts, and who absorbs it in the GL.

Keep It Honest Your own NAB Fraud Design note has the rule: what you actually built, told as fact, and how you would realise it on AWS today, told explicitly as design reasoning. Never blur "designed and defended" into "operated at scale". If you say "we ran it on MSK" the next question is your partition count and consumer rebalancing story. The split answer invites that conversation with no trap in it.
CBA: Integration CoE and BIAN (Governance Story)

How to quote your role: "I ran an Integration Centre of Excellence inside the CBA account: solutioning support, contract-first API and service-pattern guidance, platform assessments, build-versus-buy evaluations, and practice-wide technology guild sessions."

The canonical story with a diagram: canonical means one agreed definition of a business thing that every system translates to and from. A shared meaning, not a shared database. Six systems wired point-to-point need up to fifteen mappings. Through a canonical model, six.

figure 3 — fifteen versus six. That ratio is the entire business case, and it is the one number to remember
The Honest Line That Cannot Be Broken "I haven't run a BIAN programme. I've worked the problem it solves. At NAB we had two card ledgers with different data models behind one API contract, and the policy decision was consolidated so routing followed the policy outcome. I got to 'canonical at the contract, autonomous behind it' the hard way, and BIAN is where I'd go for the vocabulary."

The audit proof of concept you designed defined service boundaries, canonical data, HLD and API contracts on BIAN lines, and secured formal Security and Compliance agreement on feasibility. That is your regulated-environment governance badge.
